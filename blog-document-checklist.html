Complete USCIS Interview Document Checklist: What to Bring on Exam Day

OmniCitizen Editorial Team · Updated November 24, 2026 · 8–10 min read

Preparing your documents before a U.S. naturalization interview can make the day less stressful and help you respond clearly when a USCIS officer reviews your Form N-400. The right documents depend on your individual history and the instructions in your appointment notice.

Important: This is a practical preparation guide, not a guarantee that every item applies to every applicant. USCIS directs applicants to their interview notice and Form M-477, Document Checklist. Follow the latest official instructions for your case.

1. Core documents USCIS says to bring

USCIS lists these documents for a naturalization interview:

  • Interview appointment notice: bring the notice showing your scheduled date, time, and location.
  • Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551): commonly called your Green Card.
  • State-issued identification: for example, a driver’s license or state ID.
  • Passports and travel documents: bring all valid and expired passports and travel documents issued to you that document absences from the United States since you became a permanent resident.

These items are listed on the USCIS Naturalization: What to Expect page. Review it before your appointment in case guidance changes.

2. Additional records that may apply to your case

Not every applicant has the same history. Review your N-400 answers and Form M-477. If a topic applies to you, prepare the related original or certified records and any copies or translations requested by USCIS.

Marriage, divorce, and legal name changes

  • Marriage certificate, if relevant to your eligibility basis or application history.
  • Divorce, annulment, or death certificate documenting the end of a prior marriage, if applicable.
  • Evidence of a spouse’s U.S. citizenship if applying under the applicable three-year spouse-of-a-U.S.-citizen provision.
  • Legal name-change order or other official record if your name has changed.
  • Documents relating to a current or former spouse’s prior marriages, if requested or relevant.

Tax matters

  • Tax returns or IRS tax transcripts for relevant years, if requested or useful to clarify your circumstances.
  • Records showing a filed tax return, payment plan, or resolution of overdue taxes, if applicable.

Do not assume every applicant must bring a fixed three-to-five-year set of tax transcripts. The original version of this article stated that as a universal rule; this revised guide avoids presenting it as a requirement for everyone. Check the appointment notice, Form M-477, and your circumstances.

Arrests, citations, and court matters

  • Official court dispositions or records showing the final outcome of relevant arrests, detentions, citations, or charges, as applicable.
  • Records of probation completion or compliance, if applicable.
  • Certified copies when required. Do not assume an expunged or dismissed matter never needs to be disclosed or documented.

Other circumstances

  • Selective Service registration or status records, if relevant to your circumstances.
  • Documents about children, support obligations, or residence that relate to answers in your N-400, if requested.
  • Any document specifically requested in a USCIS notice or Request for Evidence.
  • If requesting a medical exception or accommodation, review current USCIS instructions and relevant form requirements.

3. Organize your folder before interview day

A simple folder system can help you find documents quickly. Keep originals secure and separate them from photocopies.

  1. Section A — Appointment and identity: appointment notice, Green Card, and state-issued photo ID.
  2. Section B — Travel history: current and expired passports, plus relevant USCIS-issued travel documents.
  3. Section C — Application support: a copy of the N-400 you filed and copies of supporting records you submitted.
  4. Section D — Case-specific evidence: marriage, name-change, tax, court, or other records that apply to your case.
  5. Section E — USCIS correspondence: any additional notice or request that tells you to bring specific evidence.

Practical tip: Compare your documents with the answers in the N-400 copy you retained. If something has changed since filing—such as travel, address, employment, or marital status—be prepared to answer the officer truthfully and provide relevant evidence when appropriate.

4. Documents, translations, and copies

Read the instructions that apply to each document. If you submit or present a document in a language other than English, USCIS generally requires a complete English translation with the translator’s certification as specified in its regulations and instructions. Do not translate legal documents yourself unless you meet the applicable certification requirements.

Where possible, keep a clearly labeled copy of important documents for your own reference. Bring originals when the official checklist or your appointment notice calls for them. Never mail an original document unless USCIS specifically instructs you to do so.

5. The night-before checklist

  • I checked the date, time, and location on my interview notice.
  • I packed my interview notice, Green Card, and state-issued photo ID.
  • I gathered all valid and expired passports and relevant travel documents.
  • I reviewed Form M-477 and any additional instructions from USCIS.
  • I prepared case-specific records that apply to my N-400 history.
  • I reviewed a copy of the N-400 I filed and noted any changes I must explain.
  • I arranged transportation and allowed enough time to arrive.

6. What happens at the interview?

At the naturalization interview, a USCIS officer reviews your application and background with you. Unless you qualify for an applicable exemption or waiver, you will also take the English and civics tests. The officer will explain the result and may request additional information. If your application is approved, you generally must take the Oath of Allegiance at a naturalization ceremony before you become a U.S. citizen.

For more detail, read the official USCIS interview process guide. You can also use the OmniCitizen Civics Simulator as an additional study aid, alongside official USCIS materials.
